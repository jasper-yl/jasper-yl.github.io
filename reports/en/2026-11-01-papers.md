## 1. 🔥 Hot list: Agent Context picks

### [HaMo: Harness-Model Collaboration for Long-Horizon Continual Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34724)
- Heat: HF 👍21 (on the chart 4 days, +16; was 5 at first appearance); no code repo; no X data.
- Problem: Long-horizon web agents rely either on model adaptation or on external memory, so continual learning and fast reuse trade off.
- Method: Trajectories are distilled into "strategy-level experience cards" for instant retrieval (non-parametric, fast). The model is also trained with an experience-conditioned continual-learning objective so it can interpret, ground and execute retrieved strategies (parametric, slow). "Retrieval coverage" then selects a compact, complementary set of trajectories for parameter updates.
- Result: Beats harness-only and model-only adaptation in-domain and under cross-domain transfer, improving both retention and adaptation.
- Note: Fastest-growing context paper today (+16); picked for the size of its gain.

### [Δτ-Bench: Pushing Agents Toward a Moving Capability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238) / Steward
- Heat: HF 👍24 (4 → on the chart 5 days, +4); code: [zjunlp/delta-tau-bench](https://github.com/zjunlp/delta-tau-bench) ★5.
- Problem: Tool ecosystems keep changing (tools disappear, get replaced, or gain more efficient alternatives), and existing benchmarks can't test whether an agent updates its procedural knowledge.
- Method: 1,588 multi-turn tasks across 42 domains and 6 successive ecosystem states. The authors propose Steward, which incrementally maintains skills during task execution and supplies tools and skills dynamically.
- Result: The strongest model/harness reaches only a 42.3% success rate, and the early gains of existing skill-learning methods don't last as the ecosystem evolves. Steward gets a higher success rate under ecosystem change, with lower skill-maintenance overhead.

### [ReGE: Stronger Agents through Experience Synthesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33861)
- Heat: HF 👍14 (on the chart 4 days, +1); code: [TIGER-AI-Lab/ReGE](https://github.com/TIGER-AI-Lab/ReGE) ★1.
- Problem: How to turn the execution records from earlier attempts into guidance for the next one.
- Method: ReGE-Syn makes a single coach call that jointly synthesizes a bounded execution record and its artifacts into self-contained guidance (task discoveries, suggested actions, pitfalls). The student agent starts from the initial environment state and combines the guidance with its current observation. No parameter updates and no stronger teacher.
- Result: Across four benchmarks, average accuracy rises 9.9 points for GPT and 8.8 for Claude. On BrowseComp-Plus, guidance construction uses 98.2% (GPT) / 95.5% (Claude) fewer tokens than AggAgent. Joint synthesis is clearly shorter than direct history or independent summaries.

### [Skill^∞: Recursive Self-Improvement for Agent Skill Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434)
- Heat: HF 👍8 (on the chart 5 days, +5); code: [taowangcheng/Skill-Infinity](https://github.com/taowangcheng/Skill-Infinity) ★6.
- Problem: Existing skill optimizers use hand-designed pipelines with a fixed meta-skill, and relearn from scratch for every task.
- Method: A meta agent optimizes task skills according to a meta-skill, then uses the same process to optimize the meta-skill itself. The agent diagnoses trajectories, proposes candidates, requests independent review and allocates search budget. Skills are packaged in the standard format and loaded through native skill discovery.
- Result: 60.7% pass rate on 83 SkillsBench tasks, versus 56.4% for human-written skills and 54.5% for SkillEvolver.

### [Rare, Relevant, and Still Wrong: How Memory Fails LLM Group Decisions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37624)
- Heat: HF 👍8 (on the chart 4 days, +4); no code repo.
- Problem: When a multi-LLM group chat is compressed into a short memory for the final decision-maker, what should be kept?
- Method: CrossProfile is a diagnostic benchmark (60 templates, 480 tasks) that crosses who holds the supporting, decoy and irrelevant facts while the text stays identical. It tests two retention policies: "rarest first" and "most relevant first".
- Result: Relevance filtering lets decoys in (perfect relevance filtering scores 23–31 points below rarest-first in the classic setup). Rarest-first is no better than random on average. Accuracy depends on how balanced the retained evidence is, not on the policy. The decision model shows a "social proof" bias: removing attribution labels lifts the classic setup by 13–24 points. In natural discussions, the full transcript scores lower than taking no notes.

### [DraMaze: A Multi-Agent Pipeline with Future-Aware Memory for Diverse Microdrama Script Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32002)
- Heat: HF 👍19 (on the chart 6 days, +11); no code repo.
- Problem: In long microdrama scripts (short serialized vertical-video dramas), plot mechanisms grow more concentrated. Conventional retrieval reinforces stock tropes and ignores history that matters for future plot.
- Method: Evolutionary tree search guided by trope memory, plus mutation, explores diverse causal paths. A "reverse narrative dependency tree" organizes, keeps and retrieves historical memory according to future narrative goals.
- Result: On 60-episode scripts up to 60,000 characters, repeated causal patterns drop 32.1% and Logic scores 82.5, 8.5 points above the best baseline.

### [Rethinking Verification in Agent Harnesses](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34577)
- Heat: HF 👍9 (on the chart 4 days, +0); no code repo.
- Problem: There is little guidance on how a harness should organize verification: when to step in, who checks, and how to revise.
- Method: A four-layer lifecycle framework (initial instructions, mid-run reminders, completion gate, independent review). It compares 17 designs on 7 frontier coding models (Terminal-Bench, SWE-bench Verified) and introduces VisualBench.
- Result: Independent critic review is the best value: Terminal-Bench accuracy +5.9–9.5 points at 1.13–1.25× cost, SWE-bench +3.9–6.6 at 1.00–1.04× cost. A completion gate gains more but costs noticeably more.

### [Your Policy Is Secretly a Harness: The Decision Geometry of Agent Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255)
- Heat: HF 👍8 (on the chart 4 days, +2); code: [2020-qqtcg/HRP](https://github.com/2020-qqtcg/HRP) ★0.
- Problem: What does a trained policy learn from its harness, and what is left after the harness changes?
- Method: Under KL-regularized control, the authors prove that the optimal policy's log-odds reflect the harness's preference over actions. Deployed return = harness ceiling − policy mismatch.
- Result: In MiniWoB++, merely reversing the order of observation fields drops a trained model's success rate from 85.0% to 19.2%. On Terminal-Bench Lite, "ceiling − mismatch" reproduces the accuracy ranking, and neither term alone does.

## 3. Research trend observations
- Experience and skill reuse is the hot spot for context work. [HaMo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34724) (non-parametric experience cards plus parametric adaptation), [ReGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33861) (experience synthesis, 95%+ fewer guidance-construction tokens), [Skill^∞](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35434) and [Δτ-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34238)/Steward (skill maintenance) all ask how to turn past trajectories into reusable procedural knowledge. Δτ-Bench indicates skills go stale as the environment changes.
- The harness is a research subject in its own right: verification design ([2610.34577](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34577)), policy-harness coupling ([2610.37255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37255)), and the [h1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35962) finding that an efficient interface determines cost and solve rate. All point the same way: harness and interface design matter at least as much as the model.
- Memory compression needs diagnosis. [2610.37624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37624) and [DraMaze](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32002) both show retrieval and compression policies bias results systematically (decoys, repeated tropes). More history is not automatically better.
