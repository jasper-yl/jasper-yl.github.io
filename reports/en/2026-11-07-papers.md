### [Harness-S: Learning from Sibling Harnesses for Generalizable Agent Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04277)
- Heat: HF 👍60 (1 day on the board, +9); repo [DeepExperience/Harness-S](https://github.com/DeepExperience/Harness-S) ★55; heat 61.1, first place.
- Problem: No single harness is best everywhere. Different variants fix different failures, and their usefulness shifts as the policy learns.
- Method: Starting from one base harness, it evolves a fixed pool of "sibling harnesses" along different repair directions. Only when every initial attempt under the base harness fails does it re-roll out under a chosen auxiliary harness, and those trajectories replace the failed group for RL. An Adaptive Harness Router picks the top-k using Harness Behavior Profiles plus votes from several LLMs, and recalibrates as the policy learns.
- Result: On AppWorld, SWE-Gym and DBBench, the trained policy clearly beats policy-only training when run back on the original base harness. Using the router for Rank-1 inference adds further gains at the same number of executor rollouts.

### [From Autonomous Agents to Cognitive Symbionts: Toward AI-Augmented Human Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05376)
- Heat: HF 👍40 (3 days on the board, 0 upvotes when first seen, +40); no code.
- Type: Position/vision paper by a single author, Hao Fei. No experimental data.
- Problem: People forget and miss things, and lack their own history to lean on, while an AI standing outside the situation can't help.
- Method: Proposes a "cognitive symbiont": a lifelong, embedded, real-time, private, proactive companion that keeps the user's context for years. The central concept is the "cognitive gap," the difference between what a person can bring into a situation and what working memory can hold. The design space is a loop of sense, retain, understand, co-present, act-for/think-with, and grow, wrapped in a personal runtime and trust mechanisms.
- Significance: Treats long-term personal context (multi-year memory) as the core of AI-augmented human intelligence. It also names new risks, such as covert help during exams and pressure on everyone to adopt it, and builds governance into the paradigm.

### [Switch-SWE: Do Coding Agents Hold Focus When Switching Between Unfinished Tasks?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02017)
- Heat: HF 👍9 (4 days on the board, 0 upvotes when first seen, +9); repo [Miraclecsc/Switch-SWE](https://github.com/Miraclecsc/Switch-SWE) ★26.
- Problem: After switching between several unfinished tasks in one repo, can an agent recover the earlier task's requirements and progress?
- Method: The benchmark has 200 focus tasks across 19 Python repos and 400 paired sessions. Trajectory analysis finds two recurring failures: cross-task requirement confusion and loss of task state. The authors propose a plug-in context-management module that selects task-relevant history and keeps a concise task-state summary.
- Result: Seven coding agents show an average 11.5-point resolve-rate gap (range 6.0–15.0). On OpenCode, the module raises task completion by 5.5–15.5 percentage points across models.

### [Why Pay for Long Reasoning When Rewrite Do Trick? Causal Effects of Reasoning History Compaction…](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03852)
- Heat: HF 👍8 (2 days on the board, 0 upvotes when first seen, +8); no code; single author Anton Hristov.
- Problem: Long reasoning traces are expensive. Can an open model be made to reason more briefly in later turns without retraining?
- Method: Before a tool result comes back, earlier reasoning is rewritten into a compact "state record." Continuations from the original history and the compact one are then compared.
- Result: Accepted compact histories usually shorten later reasoning, and repeated verification drops in coding cases. In software-agent deployments this can lower total cost, but task quality and wall-clock time (including the rewrite) depend on the target model and the compressor.

### [GEPA-Curator: Curated Learning for Self Improving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37651)
- Heat: HF 👍8 (4 days on the board, +3); no code.
- Problem: Failed rollouts aren't always worth learning from. They can come from prompt flaws, harness limits, randomness or noisy judges, and they mislead the prompt optimizer.
- Method: Two components. Task filtering removes noisy or unreliable tasks. Prior-success retrieval pulls past successful trajectories per task and gives them to the optimizer as extra context.
- Result: On GoBrowse, ALFWorld, AppWorld and τ-bench, with Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 5 as optimizers, the mean matches or beats baselines, up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE.

### [Can Coding Agents Sustain Continual Evolution without Forgetting?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03219)
- Heat: HF 👍7 (4 days on the board, +6); repo [THU-Agent/continual-harness-evolution](https://github.com/THU-Agent/continual-harness-evolution) ★2.
- Problem: Harness self-evolution is mostly studied on a single task. Does evolving across many tasks cause catastrophic forgetting?
- Method: An empirical study of continual evolution using two representative harness-evolution methods, over six evolution directions built from three software-engineering datasets.
- Result: Evolving on later datasets clearly hurts performance on earlier ones. With DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731, Meta-Harness reached 94.0% on SWE-Bench after evolving there, then fell to 32.0% by round 5 of evolving on FeatureBench, a 66% relative drop.

### [Can Large Language Model Agents Learn by Watching Tutorial Videos?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01460)
- Heat: HF 👍5 (4 days on the board, +3); no code.
- Problem: Can an agent learn from large numbers of tutorial videos without updating parameters?
- Method: Tutorial Agentic Learning (TAL) uses a two-stage adversarial process to extract hard, video-grounded questions and refine with them, turning videos into persistent, structured "tutorial book" memory. At inference it retrieves through a table-of-contents (TOC) tree, tying retrieval to the model's reasoning instead of embedding similarity.
- Result: On VidLearn (72 chess tutorial videos, 12 openings) it beats all baselines and improves monotonically as videos are added, with relative gains over the base model of up to 113% (train) and 91% (test). In live play, 69% of games reach a better opening position than the base model.

### [SwarmEvolve: Latent Agent Swarms for Evolutionary Discovery and Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08483)
- Heat: HF 👍6 (1 day on the board, 0 upvotes when first seen, +6); repo [Qile-J/SwarmEvolve](https://github.com/Qile-J/SwarmEvolve) ★2; found on both arXiv and HF.
- Problem: LLM-guided evolutionary search is slow. Each round generates long chains of thought, repeated many times to keep the population diverse.
- Method: Each round, hundreds of cheap agents work as a swarm, reasoning in latent space without decoding to text and sharing information by passing KV cache. Novelty filtering uses distances between hidden-state vectors. An aggregator agent receives the merged KV cache of all members and does idea crossover in latent space.
- Result: Highest average score across 21 math-discovery and scientific-computing problems. It reaches the best score other SOTA frameworks need 60 minutes for in a median of 3–8 minutes. On some problems, Qwen3 8B beats the solutions AlphaEvolve first found with frontier LLMs.
